1Get the words

Every good post starts with something you watched.

You're scrolling YouTube on the bus, or in your lunch break, and a video stops you. A clear idea, a great story, a point your buyers need to hear. It would make a brilliant post, in your own words, for your own people.

But a video is sound, not words. To write about it you'd have to watch it again with a notepad, pause every ten seconds, and type. On a phone, nobody does that. The idea is gone by tomorrow.
So skip the typing. Tap Share on the video, choose Get Transcript, and a few seconds later every word is there, with the title and the link. It works for YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and X, and for your own voice notes and screen recordings too.

Try it now

  1. Open a video in YouTube and tap Share.
  2. Swipe the row of apps to the right, tap More, and choose Get Transcript.
  3. The first time, tap Edit and the green + next to Get Transcript, so it sits at the front of the row from now on.
  4. Wait a few seconds. You'll see how many words it found, with Read it all and Copy it.

2Hand them to ChatGPT

The words are only as good as what you ask for.

Under the words sits a list of ready-made instructions for faceless text posts, the kind you post without ever showing your face: a Facebook post in Bangla and English, a post with an audience check, a story with a character and a villain, a thread, a comment, a summary, the key points. Pick one, and the instruction plus the whole transcript go to ChatGPT together.

Copying, switching apps, pasting the transcript, then typing a good prompt from memory: that's where most people give up, or end up with a bland "here's a summary" post that sounds like everyone else's.
One tap does all of it. Tap the instruction you want: everything is copied and ChatGPT opens. Tap the message box, Paste, and send. The post prompts even make ChatGPT score its own work out of 10, and rewrite it until it's happy with it.

Your first post

  1. Under Ask, keep ChatGPT picked (or switch to Claude).
  2. Tap FB post (Bangla, 99), or any other instruction.
  3. In ChatGPT, tap the message box, Paste, then the send arrow.
  4. Read what it wrote. AI can be wrong, so you're the editor. Like it? Copy it and post.

Have instructions of your own that work for you? Save them in My prompts with a name. They show at the top of the list after every transcript, on your phone and on this website.

3A thread from any video

One video, a whole week of content.

A thread keeps people reading: one strong opening post, then a comment after comment that each adds one more idea. It's one of the best ways to stay in front of people on Facebook and X.

Writing one by hand means splitting the idea into pieces, keeping each piece short, and making them flow. It's an hour of work for one post.
Choose Thread post (X + Facebook) instead. ChatGPT writes an X version first, then a Facebook version: the main post plus every comment, each in its own box with a copy button. The last comment is your call to action.

4Posts with no video

A post of words has no transcript to get.

Some of the best things on Facebook, LinkedIn and Threads are written posts, and the gold is often in the comments under them. Share one of those to Get Transcript, and it knows there's nothing to transcribe.

The usual fix is screenshots: one, scroll, another, scroll, another. Then you pick them out of your gallery in the right order, attach them to ChatGPT one by one, and hope it can read them all.
Get Transcript records it instead. It offers Record this post. You scroll through the post and the comments you want, and stop. It joins the recording into one long picture, cuts it into screen-sized pages, and reads every word on them. The pages also go into your Photos.

Record a post

  1. On the post, tap Share → Get Transcript → Record this post.
  2. iPhone: tap Start Broadcast, then ◀ at the top left to go back to the post. Android: tap Start.
  3. Scroll down slowly through the post and the comments you want.
  4. iPhone: tap the red time at the top, then Stop. Android: pull down the notifications and tap Stop.
  5. Tap the "Post recorded" note. The post is read and ready.

No Share button handy? On the app's home screen, tap Capture a text post: record and scroll, start recording, and open the post yourself.

5A comment that brings people back

The cheapest leads are under other people's posts.

Big creators pull thousands of people into their comment sections every day. Those people are already interested, and already reading. Show up there with a comment that adds something real, and some of them click on you, read your profile and message you. You're borrowing traffic, and it costs you nothing.

Borrow it like a good guest, though. A "DM me", a link, a pitch, and the creator bans you. And a genuinely helpful comment takes thought: read the whole post, read what others said, find something worth adding. After a full day at work, doing that well three times a day is exactly what never happens.
After a post is read, Comment on this post sits at the top of the list. Tap it, Paste in ChatGPT, send. It has the whole post and the comments as text, so you don't attach a single screenshot. It writes a humble, supportive comment that adds value, checks its own quality, and puts the post's link on the first line, so you can always find the post again.

From post to comment

  1. Record the post (chapter 4), then tap "Post recorded".
  2. Tap Comment on this post.
  3. In ChatGPT: Paste, send. Read the comment it writes.
  4. Copy it, go back to the post (or tap the link), paste and post.

Want to see the whole post as text first? Tap Read it all. You can select and copy any part of it.

6Save it for later

The best ideas show up when you're busy.

You're scrolling in a queue, between meetings, half-watching. You see a video or a post you'd love to use. Right now you can't stop and write.

Screenshots and "I'll remember it" don't work. By tonight it's buried under a hundred other things, and Facebook won't show you that post again.
Tap Share → Save for later. One tap, a quick "Saved", and you're back to scrolling. When you have time, open the Saved tab: everything you kept is waiting. Tap one to get its words and write your post. Mark it done when you've used it.

Keep it in one tap

  1. Tap Share on a post or video.
  2. Choose Save for later in the list under the app icons (tap View More the first time).
  3. Later: open Get Transcript → Saved.

Is it really free?

Yes. YouTube videos have no limit. Posts, voice notes and other videos can be read up to 20 times a day.

Do I need ChatGPT Plus?

No. The free ChatGPT app works. You can also pick Claude instead, or tap Copy it and paste the words anywhere you like.

Does my screen recording get uploaded?

No. The recording stays on your phone and is turned into pages there. Only those pages (pictures of the post) are sent to be read, and the pages are also saved in your Photos.

Which apps does it work with?

Videos from YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, Facebook and X, plus voice notes and recordings from your phone. Record this post works for written posts on Facebook, LinkedIn and Threads, and for anything else you can scroll through.

Will ChatGPT get the post right?

Most of the time, and the prompts make it check and score its own work. But AI can be wrong, so always read what it wrote before you post it. You're the editor.

I used the old Share button shortcut or this website before. What changed?

The app replaces the shortcut, and everything you had still works. The website version is still here: tap Web app at the top of this page.
